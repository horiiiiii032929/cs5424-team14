\section{Task 1: Baseline Design for Workload A}
\label{sec:baseline-a}

\subsection{\expt{a0}: Experimental setup}
We evaluated the supplied baseline using PostgreSQL 18.6 and Citus 14.1.0 on
the SoC compute cluster through Slurm. The cluster consisted of one coordinator
and four workers, with four CPUs allocated per node. Principal settings were
\code{shared_buffers = 1GB}, \code{work_mem = 32MB}, and an adaptive executor
pool limit of four.

Full A experiments used scale 1, \code{SERIALIZABLE} isolation, a 60-second
warmup and a 600-second measurement, at 1, 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64 concurrent clients.
Transaction logs used full sampling. At scale 1, the initial dataset contained
1,000 merchants, one million customers, 500,000 products, 500,000 inventory
records, five million orders and 15 million order items. Approximately half
of order activity was directed to 10\% of merchants \citep{project-brief}.

Writes account for 75\% of the mix, but frequency alone does not identify the
bottleneck. The baseline distributes the five distributed tables by their own
identifiers. In particular, \code{orders}, \code{order_items} and
\code{inventory} use \code{order_id}, \code{order_item_id} and
\code{inventory_id}, respectively. Related rows are therefore not necessarily
colocated according to important join keys.

We report TPS as successfully logged completed scripts divided by the configured
measurement duration. Completed scripts can include expected business rollbacks;
this is not committed orders per second. Percentiles describe successfully
logged transactions, while failures and retries are reported separately
\citep{pgbench}.

\subsection{\expt{a0}: Baseline results}
The preserved baseline ran as job 918524, with xcnf8 as coordinator and four
xcnf workers. Table~\ref{tab:a-baseline} shows that throughput did not scale
with concurrency, while p95 latency reached approximately 180 seconds at
64 clients. All measured pgbench processes exited normally, but the last three
client counts recorded serialization failures. A zero process exit did not
mean that every transaction succeeded.

At one client, T3 averaged 2,678.797\,ms, T4 458.862\,ms, and T7 51.114\,ms.
Multiplying each transaction's completed count by its mean latency, T3 and T4
together accounted for approximately 93.6\% of recorded time at one client
and 97.2\% at 64. These are shares of accumulated transaction latency, not CPU
utilization or workload weights. They identify order history and product lookup
as the main delays experienced by clients.

\subsection{\expt{a0}: Fresh baseline control}
We reran the unmodified baseline as job 977740 on xcnf0--4, the same nodes later
used for a112. At the six client counts, it achieved
3.225/3.565/3.568/3.282/3.613/3.222 TPS, with
0/0/2/20/64/59 serialization failures. All phases exited normally.

Its one-client result closely matched the preserved baseline, but throughput
at four or more clients was approximately 39--56\% higher. The cause was not
established. This variation shows why separate cluster runs cannot automatically
attribute every difference to an optimization. Section~\ref{sec:a112-full}
therefore compares a112 with this fresh control, retaining its failure counts.
